The challenge

National DHIS2 instances accumulate years of customizations — program stages, validation rules, dashboards, integrations — built up over multiple administrations and multiple versions of the platform. Upgrading isn't a routine update; it's a project where a missed breaking change can quietly corrupt reporting for an entire program.

Programs needed a path to newer DHIS2 versions without interrupting the reporting cycles that health teams depend on week to week.

The approach

I led national malaria programs through complex DHIS2 upgrades by treating each one as a structured migration rather than a simple version bump.

  • Audited release notes for breaking changes against each instance's customizations
  • Built staging environments to test upgrades before touching production
  • Validated metadata, program rules, and dashboards post-upgrade
  • Planned and executed phased deployment to minimize disruption

The outcome

Programs moved onto current, supported DHIS2 versions with no loss of historical data or reporting continuity — giving them access to newer platform features and security patches without the downtime or data risk that ad hoc upgrades typically carry.

Required close coordination with program teams to schedule upgrade windows around active reporting cycles and campaigns.
